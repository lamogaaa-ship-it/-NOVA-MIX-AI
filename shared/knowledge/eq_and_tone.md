# EQ and tone

## Subtractive before additive
Removing what masks the sound (mud, boxiness, resonances) often achieves more clarity than boosting. Narrow cuts (Q 4-8) are for resonances; broad moves (Q 0.5-1.5) are for tonal balance.

## Tonal regions (typical, always verify)
Sub 20-60 Hz: rumble on vocals, weight on masters. Low 60-250 Hz: body, boom, proximity. Low-mid 250-500 Hz: warmth when balanced, mud when excessive. Mid 500-2k: honk, nasality, intelligibility of vowels. Presence 2-5 kHz: clarity and forwardness, harshness when excessive. Sibilance 5-10 kHz. Air 10-20 kHz: openness.

## Dynamic EQ
A dynamic EQ band only cuts (or boosts) when the band exceeds a threshold. It is the right tool when a problem only exists at certain moments (harsh notes, boomy notes, resonances that ring only when loud).

## Loudness bias
Louder almost always sounds "better" in A/B tests. Every tonal comparison must be loudness matched; otherwise a boost wins simply because it is louder.

## Warmth
Warmth combines low-mid body, a slightly softened top end and gentle harmonic saturation (tape-like even/odd harmonics). Too much low-mid becomes mud.
