# Space: reverb and delay

## Reverb as a return
Reverb and delay are usually used as sends so the dry signal stays intact. High-pass (200-300 Hz) the reverb return to avoid low-end mud, and damp its high end so it does not compete with sibilance.

## Pre-delay and ducking
Pre-delay separates the direct sound from the reverb onset and keeps words intelligible. Ducking the effect returns with the dry signal lets the space bloom in the gaps.

## Tempo-synced delay
Delay times derived from the song tempo sit rhythmically: quarter note = 60000/BPM ms, eighth = half of that, dotted eighth = 0.75 of a quarter, triplet eighth = 2/3 of an eighth. Ping-pong delays widen a mono vocal without moving the lead from the centre.

## Measuring space
The level of the sound 50-100 ms after a phrase ends, relative to the phrase level, indicates how much reverb (or room) is present; the decay slope after phrase offsets estimates the decay time. Reverb in a recording cannot be removed with EQ or compression.
