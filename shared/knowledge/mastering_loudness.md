# Mastering and loudness

## Measurement
Loudness is measured in LUFS (ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R128): integrated (whole programme, gated), short-term (3 s) and momentary (400 ms). Loudness range (LRA, EBU Tech 3342) describes macro dynamics. True peak is measured with oversampling and is typically kept at or below -1 dBTP for delivery.

## Typical targets
Streaming platforms normalise around -14 LUFS integrated (some -16 to -13). Commercially loud modern masters often sit around -10 to -7 LUFS integrated at the cost of dynamics. There is no single correct number: louder masters are turned down by streaming normalisation.

## Preserving punch
Pushing a limiter harder lowers the peak-to-loudness ratio (PLR). A PLR below roughly 7-8 dB usually sounds squashed on dynamic material. Gentle bus compression (1-2 dB gain reduction, slow attack) before the limiter spreads the work and keeps transients.

## Mastering chain
Corrective EQ, dynamic resonance control, gentle compression, tonal shaping, saturation, stereo management (mono bass below ~120 Hz), clipping and limiting - but only the steps the track needs.

## Stereo and mono
Check correlation and the mono sum. Wide low end causes mono and vinyl problems; keep sub/bass centred. Negative correlation means phase cancellation in mono.
