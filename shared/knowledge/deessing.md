# De-essing

## Finding the frequency
The sibilance frequency is voice specific (roughly 4.5-10 kHz). Centre the de-esser on the measured sibilant peak rather than a preset value.

## Split band vs wide band
Split-band de-essing reduces only the sibilant region, keeping the rest of the voice untouched. Wide-band de-essing turns down the whole signal during the 's' and is more transparent on some voices but can duck the vowel that follows.

## Amount
Typical reduction on sibilants is 3-8 dB. Beyond about 10-12 dB the voice starts to sound lispy. Boosting presence or air increases sibilance, so de-essing belongs after tonal boosts, and the result should be verified on the actual sibilant moments while checking air on the rest of the vocal.
